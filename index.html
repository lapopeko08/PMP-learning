Section 2. A System for Value Delivery

2.1 Creating value

Business value is a net quantifiable benefit, categorized into tangible and intangible

Tangible: Monetary assests, Productivity, Infrastructure improvements, etc.

Intangible: Brand recognition & trademark, public benefit, reputation

Value Delivery System

Component: Vision > Mission > Organizational Strategy & Objective > Portfolio > Programs > Project & Operation

Connection: Each component produce outcome. Outcome produce benefit (or disbenefit). Benefit creates value.

Project success

Dimension 1: Project outcome – how effectiveness project realizes value.

Dimension 2: Project management processes – how well project adhere to constraints (cost, scope, time, quality)

2.2 Project Environment

Two categories of influences: EEFs – enterprise environmental factors and OPAs – organizational process assets.

EEF External Marketplace conditions, social influences, Regulations, etc.
Internal Organizational cuture, Geographic distribution, Resource capability, Financial capability
OPA Process, Policies, Procedures
Knowledge Repo

2.3 Product management 

Integrate of people, data, processes, business system to create, develop, maintain a product or a service. 

PdM - provide vision and strategy for product 

PgM / PjM - ensure the vision is brought to life by managing execution, dependencies & resources. structuring & guiding the development process, risk, ensure alignment with organizational 

PO - ensure team focus on delivering valuable features by managing/prioritizing backlog 

BA - gather and document requirements, ensure project scope align biz needs


2.5 Project Management Roles

Bringing focus & clarity to the work 

Manage needs and task effectively 

Prioritize & allocate attention 

Competencies

 - Social responsibilities: project aligned common good 

- Power skills: critical thinking, decision making, imp team motivation methods, negotiation & conflict resolution 

- Business acumen: skill in making correct decision by understanding the business 

- Ways of working: appropriate project management approach, brainstorm techniques

- Results: ability to deal with enterprise politics

Accountable Leader

Projects may involve multiple departments, vendor, org, that do not interact on regular basis; carry higher stakes and expectations than regular operational functions. => Broader array of stakeholders attempt to influence project. Diversity of influence cause confusion and conflict.

So, accountable leadership = responsible & own project target business objectives, actions taken, decisions made. 

- Influence member

- Accountable for member actions 

- Effectively lead by examples

- Effectively adapt style 

- Foster an environment of safety, collaboration

Integrate Sustainability 

Sustainability = meeting present needs without harming future generations need

Consider strategy to address any externality that project may create

- Compensate/Offset for negative outcomes generated

- Restore impacts

- Minimize negative outcomes

- Avoid negative outcomes altogether

The section explores project life cycle through 5 factors

4.1 Project Phases

Can be: sequential, transitional, overlapping

Product Lifecycle can be part of the outcomes produced by a project; hence # Project lifecycle

Phases: name, number, duration, resource requirements, entrance&exit criteria, phase gate & pre-phase gate

4.2 Project Development Approaches

Defines how the project will be managed and executed

Predictive / Adaptive / Hybrid 

Requirement clear & fixed = Predictive

Requirement less clear = Adaptive, either fixed scope or fixed timeline/budget

4.2.1 Predictive Approaches

- Optimal for

  • projects where cost of integrate exceeds its value
  • heavily regulated env

- Can have incremental/partial delivery

- Incremental: Requirements are clear. You ship: increment 1 = product catalog, increment 2 = shopping cart, increment 3 = checkout. Each is complete and usable when delivered; you're not expecting the plan to change much.

- Adaptive: You start with a prioritized backlog, deliver small pieces every 2-week sprint, show them to the customer, and re-plan — maybe after seeing the cart, the customer changes direction, adds new needs, reprioritizes. Requirements evolve as you go. 

4.2.2 Adaptive Approaches

- Optimal for : Requirement & solutions are subject to high level of uncertainty; likely to change significantly throughout the project

  • Design projects
  • New solution/product development
  • Digital projects

- Required : A clear vision, and initial known requirement

- Can be iterative & incremental

- Popular method : flow-based scheduling, e.g Kanban

4.3.3 Hybrid approaches

- Optimal for : 

  • Uncertainty / risk around the requirements, so iterate designing part, once locked develop using predictive
  • Deliverables can be developed by different project teams
  • Project team has 2 main deliverables, each can have different approach 

Today, most projects require hybrid approach, to address diverse challenges and complexities

4.3 Considerations for Approach

- Deliverables : Requirement certainty, Scope stability, Ease of change, Safety/Regulations/Risk

- Org structure : 

- Project : Stakeholders, Schedule constraint, Team size & Exp, Interdependencies

- Delivery Cadence : Single, Multiple, Periodic, Continuous 

4.5 Project Management Focus area

Describes fundamental actions take place over the course of any project, regardless of approach being taken : Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring & Closing. 

4.5.1 Initiating 

- Processes/Practice to define project, or new phase of an existing project

- Purpose: align stakeholder expectations, project purpose, scope & objective 

- Input: Biz document

- Output: Project charter, initial scope is defined, resources are committed

4.5.2 Planning

- Processes/Practices to establish scope of effort, define/refine objectives, course of activity. E.g : high level roadmap 

- Output: course of activity required to complete project

4.5.3 Executing

- Apply development approach to execute & delivery

4.5.4 Monitoring & Controlling

- Monitoring project health, and controlling changes 

- Purpose: ensure project is on track & meet expectations

prOject performance domain

Overview

  • Group of related processes ; work in unison to achieve desired project results
  • 7 domains: Governance, Scope, Schedule, Finance, Stakeholders, Resources and Risks


Governance Performance Domain 

- Consists of Framework, Functions, Processes

- Identify, define, combine, unify and coordinate various processes, documents, and pm activities as an integrated system

- Important to apply suitable governance method - not too much nor too little

- 2 approaches: 

  • Structured Governance : Prj sponsor, PMO, PjM 
  • Self - Governance : management distribute among team member ; key challenges is fragmented decision-making

- Effective Governance required

  • Target metrics - KPI
  • Clear & effective alarm system for above metrics
  • Effective feedback
